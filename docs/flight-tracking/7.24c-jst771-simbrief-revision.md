# JST771: SimBrief revision history and future revision design

Status: evidence analysis and future design only. No synchronization, revision workflow, or production behavior is implemented by this document. This does not start 7.25 or premium UI work.

## Authoritative clarification and finding

The pilot clarifies that the original accepted SimBrief OFP specified FL330. They reissued it to change runway/routing, did not notice that the new release also changed cruise altitude to FL350, and flew FL330. This explains the planned-versus-observed altitude discrepancy without implying aircraft identity corruption or claiming a recovery reset caused it.

Local historical evidence corroborates that account. The first assignment contains FL330 and runway 23; the subsequent assignment contains FL350 and runway 05. Actual flight telemetry levels near pressure altitude 33,000 ft, then descends. The original and revised OFPs also differ in cost index, fuel and predicted times. The application was therefore evaluating actual FL330 flight against the revised FL350 plan.

The separate Top-of-Descent detection defect remains: `FlightTrackingEvents.cs:91-105` requires cruise within 600 ft of the current filed level before descent can qualify. No sample qualified at FL350. Sustained actual level flight at FL330 and the subsequent descent were sufficient operational evidence, but the detector did not use that evidence independently of the revised plan. Correct revision awareness would explain the mismatch to the pilot; it must not substitute for robust observation of flight phase.

## Future model: accepted baseline, proposed revisions and observed flight

The following is a future design specification, not the current implementation.

Maintain four explicitly different records tied to one stable operational flight ID:

1. **Original accepted release, REV0.** Immutable snapshot of the release first accepted for the flight, with original raw response/document references, normalized material fields, source generation identity, generation/fetch/acceptance timestamps and content hash. Future REV0 is an application revision label, independent of SimBrief's OFP 1 or OFP 2 number.
2. **Latest proposed release.** Newly fetched SimBrief data is a proposal until the pilot applies it. Fetching, reopening Dispatch or restarting the application does not change the current accepted plan. Retain proposal identity and provenance; a later fetch must not silently replace the proposal a pilot is currently reviewing.
3. **Current accepted release, REVn.** An immutable revision plus a current-pointer and an append-only acceptance record. Record its parent accepted revision and exact reviewed diff. REV0 always remains accessible. Reapplying an identical revision is idempotent. If acceptance state changed during review, recalculate the diff before applying.
4. **Actual observed flight.** Telemetry, observed level/phase, path, OUT/OFF/ON/IN, aircraft observations, fuel measurements and manual confirmations. Planned values must never overwrite actuals. Show actual FL330 separately from current accepted FL350; preserve a deliberate pilot-entered operating intention or exception with provenance if supported in future.

The material diff must cover origin/destination and flight identity; route text and coordinate path; SID, STAR, transitions and departure branches; arrival approach and transition where supplied; departure/destination/alternate runways; cruise and step-climb levels; cost index and climb/cruise/descent profiles; trip, taxi, contingency, alternate, reserve, extra, takeoff and block fuel with units; destination, takeoff and en-route alternates; aircraft type/variant, registration, equipment and performance profile; payload/passengers/weights; scheduled OUT/IN, estimated OUT/OFF/ON/IN, taxi and block times; route distance, gates and dispatch remarks. Include source date/AIRAC for procedure comparison.

Show both old and proposed values, units and meaningful deltas. Explicitly distinguish **changed**, **unchanged**, **not supplied**, and **unverified**. Missing values must not silently erase previously accepted fields. Cosmetic whitespace and proven identifier aliases can be normalized; preserve original provider values alongside normalized ones. A change to an airport/flight identity or aircraft observation must enter a deliberate identity/operational review, not relabel historical actuals as a different flight.

## Future pilot application and preservation guarantees

Refresh should produce a clear revision summary such as: “Runway 23 → 05; departure path changed; cruise FL330 → FL350; CI24 → CI18; block fuel 7,306 → 7,223 kg.” The pilot can inspect the complete diff and explicitly **Apply revision to this flight** or retain the current release. Acknowledging a runway change alone is not authorization to silently replace cruise, fuel or performance fields. If future field-specific acceptance is offered, retain an explicit override ledger and identify the resulting plan as a composite; do not present it as an untouched SimBrief release.

Applying a same-flight revision must use a dedicated operation, not today's `AcceptAssignment` replacement path (`FlightSessionCoordinator.Commands.cs:10-20`), which requires disconnection, deletes recovery state and resets tracking/identity. Future application must:

- Preserve the same operational flight/history ID, session and assignment lineage, recorded OUT/OFF/ON/IN, event history, actual flown path, engine/terminal evidence, statistics and exactly-once PIREP identity.
- Append the revision acceptance and update the current accepted pointer atomically. A failed save leaves the previous accepted revision and recovery checkpoint usable; a restart recovers one accepted pointer and never silently applies a pending proposal.
- Recompute navigation/remaining distance from fresh position against the newly accepted route without resetting recorded distance flown or substituting the original route denominator. Record any expected change in geographical progress so a revision is distinguishable from a tracking failure.
- Recompute estimates from current telemetry and the accepted plan while preserving elapsed-flight estimator context. Do not restart the schedule blend, manufacture actual times, or recalculate completed historical statistics against a new plan.
- Preserve the observation-based phase detector independently of plan mismatch. An accepted FL350 plan does not negate sustained observed level flight at FL330 or prevent detecting actual descent.
- Keep terminal authority based on fresh simulator capability and physical evidence. Applying an OFP revision must not authorize engine shutdown, block-in, PIREP submission or change an existing confirmation.
- On PIREP, record references to REV0 and the final accepted revision plus revision history where useful, while keeping observed actuals and the single operational-flight identity authoritative. A completed/submitted report is not silently rewritten by a later fetch.

Future regression coverage should include this exact FL330 → FL350 runway-driven reissue with actual FL330 flight; same-route altitude/CI/fuel-only revisions; unchanged refresh; missing fields and unit changes; a pending proposal across disconnect/restart; same-flight application before OUT and after OFF; save failure/retry; material identity change requiring review; arrival-route changes near destination without false 100%; retained observed descent; and one final PIREP with unchanged historical OOOI.

No new native flight is requested for this design document. The historical evidence assertion script is separate: `scripts/test-jst771-evidence.py` verifies JST771's observed unsupported T1/manual-completion outcome and must not be presented as proof of a newly successful native adapter or implemented revision workflow.
