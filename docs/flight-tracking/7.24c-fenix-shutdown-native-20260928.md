Reported fuel flow takes **49.985 seconds after the observed Engine 2 master-OFF** and **51.015 seconds after Engine 1 master-OFF** to meet the current classifier's ≤1 lb/h condition. Both masters and combustion indications are OFF by 39.203 seconds, but both fuel flows do not satisfy that condition until 89.203 seconds: **50 additional seconds**. Both N2 readings already meet the mechanical Off bound by 66.203 seconds, 23 seconds earlier than the fuel-flow requirement.

This independently reproduces the kind of residual-flow delay identified in JST776. It supports separating terminal shutdown intent from mechanical engine classification. It does not measure an IN or PIREP improvement: Sierra was not participating in this capture, and no ON, assignment/destination validation, IN, completion or PIREP event is present. Current engine/phase confirmation timers would add further conditions; do not label the 50-second interval an observed PIREP latency.

## Capability decision and next implementation boundary

The capture is sufficient to advance **a narrowly scoped Fenix A321 IAE intent adapter proposal** using `L:S_ENG_MASTER_1/2` plus corroborating combustion. No additional full flight is needed to discover these variables. Scope this evidence to the observed title/package; do not automatically assert equivalent behavior for the A220, other Fenix variants/versions or four-engine aircraft.

It is not sufficient on its own to declare the whole automatic-completion policy safe. It records one ordered shutdown and does not exercise relight, startup already OFF, missing/unsupported LVars, injected failure/fuel starvation, reconnect or restart. Constant-zero starter/failure signals establish no independent failure-detection capability. Their lack of transitions must not be treated as proof that those optional signals work.

Recommended next implementation scope, consistent with the prior design:

1. Add completion-specific, immutable shutdown evidence at the simulator boundary, carrying source/capability, engine coverage, receipt time, simulator time and connection/flight identity. Preserve the generic engine classifier and events.
2. Map the observed Fenix master channels to explicit intent only for a positively identified supported configuration. Successful registration or fresh zeroes alone must not establish support. Define startup and reconnect qualification explicitly; absence must remain unavailable rather than default OFF.
3. Require observed ON/TaxiIn, valid destination, stationary/on-ground/brake, all required master cutoffs and absent combustion, fresh coherent data and the existing short Complete confirmation. Reset on movement, brake release, relight, pause/slew, time discontinuity, stale/contradictory evidence or connection/session change. Residual reported spool/fuel flow alone must not veto otherwise verified intent.
4. Harden manual Block In through the shared UI/command eligibility predicate identified in the prior report. Unsupported capability and temporarily invalid evidence remain distinct. Preserve single IN, original completed-history identity and idempotent PIREP.
5. Verify nominal two-/four-engine fixtures, single-engine taxi, partial/missing coverage, failure/starvation with masters still ON, invalid zeros, wrong destination/missing ON, interruptions and recovery/retries. Preserve the historical replay interpretation; absent legacy intent fields must not be manufactured.

This review adds evidence and analysis only. No production adapter, automatic authority, timer change or fallback behavior was implemented. **GO for capability-informed implementation design and regression work; NO-GO for checkpoint closure or airborne validation.** The new map scheduling correction still requires its separate frozen-build/installed verification; this capture does not validate it.
